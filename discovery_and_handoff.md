## BANT discovery worksheet
Budget: Is funding allocated? Who controls approval? Record unknown rather than guessing.
Authority: Who evaluates, approves and signs? Is the current contact an evaluator or decision-maker?
Need: What problem does the prospect describe? What is its impact and current workaround?
Timeline: Is there an evaluation deadline, renewal or planned project?
Next step: Action, owner, date, meeting participants and agreed agenda.

## Fictional role-play A: continue discovery
A healthcare IT manager reports inconsistent training across clinics. Budget, approval authority and timing are unknown. Ask who owns the awareness programme, how gaps are measured and whether a review is planned. Do not label this an SQL.

## Fictional role-play B: candidate for sales acceptance
A manufacturing security lead reports inconsistent onboarding, confirms an approved training budget and an evaluation planned within 60 days, and agrees to include the approving stakeholder in discovery. These facts are invented for role-play. Apply the employer's qualification criteria before classifying an SQL.

## Sample handoff for role-play B
Account: Fictional manufacturing organization.

Current process: Local onboarding sessions; measurement details unknown.
Budget: Approved in this scenario; amount unknown.
Authority: Security lead evaluates; approving stakeholder joins next call.

Next step: Discovery with product specialist; confirm participants and date.
Open questions: Number of users, training languages, current tools, procurement requirements and success measures.
No promises: Do not claim product capabilities or security outcomes without approved material.

## Metrics example (invented numbers)
100 delivered emails; 8 replies; 3 positive replies; 2 booked meetings; 1 held meeting; 1 sales-accepted lead.
Reply rate: 8/100 = 8%.
Positive reply rate: 3/100 = 3%.
Booked meeting rate: 2/100 = 2%.
Attendance rate: 1/2 = 50%.
Sales acceptance: 1/1 = 100%, with too small a sample for meaningful inference.
Separate attempted, delivered, replied, booked, held and sales-accepted stages. Never present these figures as candidate results.
